How the agent should work

Six pictures of the finished system for PPC’s dynamic export limits: the loop, one 30-minute cycle, what each house receives, how the model is kept honest, where the money comes from and where it grows next. Everything is a simulation.

The closed loop

Meters feed a forecast, the agent turns it into a limit per house, a guard and a person check it, and the inverters apply it. The new readings start the next cycle. Today the loop is closed on replayed Ausgrid data; the dashed part needs the operator.

One 30-minute cycle

Who talks to whom, in order. The model sees only what existed an hour before the interval starts. Dashed lines are answers and the parts that do not exist yet.

What limit each house gets

The model’s limit applies only where it is sure, and never above the contract cap. Example from the contract file: 52% from the cautious quantile, three houses.

How the model stays honest

Training, the abstention threshold and the final test use different slices of time. Monitoring and recalibration after deployment are the missing half.

Where the value comes from

A safe higher limit means more exported energy. The pricing layer on main turns that into money: an hourly price for prosumers, a battery that sells in the evening, and less imbalance from a better forecast.

Where it grows next

The transformer station is the natural unit: it sees the sum of everything below it. Export limits are the first of three levers, ordered by what they cost.
